Evaluation — Sep 2026

CHAI — Lenacapavir (LEN) HIV Prevention

CHAI proposes technical assistance to accelerate LEN PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis — HIV prevention drugs taken by HIV-negative people at high risk) rollout across SSA (Sub-Saharan Africa), claiming $68/DALY (Disability-Adjusted Life Year — one year of healthy life lost) via a 2.15× co-financing leverage multiplier. 7 deep-research runs, Sep 22 2026 (4 additional pending). Prior deep evaluation runs from Aug 2026.

Provisional verdict

Do not fund at $68/DALY headline. Fund as market-shaping bet at ~$3–5M if appropriate.

The $68/DALY CE figure is ~67% leverage artifact. The 2.15× co-financing multiplier — the single most consequential input in CHAI's model — has no empirical basis: zero documented cases of CHAI TA causing government domestic budget allocation for LEN, and every African country's LEN procurement runs through Global Fund or PEPFAR, not domestic treasuries. Without the leverage claim, CE is $165–608/DALY depending on what acceleration credit is given.

CHAI provides genuine value — primarily through the Dr Reddy's generic deal ($40/person-year across 120 LMICs) and country-level regulatory/implementation TA. These are real market-shaping contributions worth pricing as acceleration, not co-financing. At realistic leverage (~0.2×), CE is ~$250–350/DALY: better than cash transfers, comparable to other market-shaping investments, but 3–5× above the GiveWell bar.

The post-USAID environment worsens the picture: the donor pool CHAI claims to leverage is shrinking, and PEPFAR's retrenchment (PEPFAR is the US government's global HIV/AIDS funding program, cut ~70% since 2025) further undermines the Pathway 2B mechanism.

Context and model

Lenacapavir (LEN) is a twice-yearly injectable HIV capsid inhibitor. PURPOSE 1 and 2 trials showed 96–100% HIV prevention efficacy. As of September 2026, ~66,000 people have initiated LEN PrEP across 9 African countries (South Africa 55K+ initiations). The 2026 target is 600,000+ initiations. Dr Reddy's has a licensed generic at $40/person-year across 120 LMICs.

CHAI's proposal structures the grant as two pathways:

  • Pathway 1 (direct): Grant-funded delivery and TA — direct CE ~$608/DALY without leverage
  • Pathway 2 (leverage): CHAI's technical assistance is claimed to unlock government and donor co-financing via a 2.15× permanent multiplier — meaning every $1 CHAI spends triggers $2.15 in additional funding from others — claimed CE ~$68/DALY blended

Two-thirds of the headline CE comes from Pathway 2 — the leverage claim. That's what this evaluation stress-tests.

Pathway 2 leverage — the decisive issue

Run: LEN_Pathway2_leverage_model_validity — 3 phases, Sep 22 2026

Core finding
The 2.15× leverage claim is not credible. Every African country funds LEN through GF/PEPFAR, not domestic treasuries. [run] Pathway 2A (government leverage) contributes approximately zero. Pathway 2B (donor acceleration) is real but worth ~0.1–0.4×, not 2.15×.

Pathway 2A — government domestic funding: zero cases

CHAI claims TA causes governments to allocate domestic budget for LEN procurement. As of September 2026, there are zero documented cases of this occurring. The evidence:

  • South Africa's R513M ($29.2M) LEN procurement: Global Fund (GF) grant redirected within existing envelope, not Treasury allocation
  • Kenya's 21,000 starter doses: Global Fund-funded
  • All 8+ early-adopter African countries: procurement through GF or PEPFAR grants
  • LEN's drug cost is $40/person-year — within-envelope substitution, not additionality

Pathway 2B — donor leverage: real but modest

CHAI co-brokered the Dr Reddy's generic deal ($40/PY across 120 LMICs with Unitaid/Wits RHI), provides country-level TA, and has real influence in the ecosystem. But the major funding commitments were made independently: Gilead's at-cost supply, PEPFAR's commitment, GF's 8th Replenishment priority designation, and BMGF's Hetero generic deal were all made without CHAI attribution in their own announcements. At least 10 major organizations claim credit for the same rollout.

The honest characterization: CHAI provides acceleration (things happen 6–18 months faster) not causation. This is worth pricing — but at 0.1–0.4×, not 2.15×.

Why the empirical base rate is so far below 2.15×

The best empirical evidence on HIV aid leverage across 134 countries (2000–2015) finds a leverage ratio of 0.12:1 — CHAI's 2.15× is ~18× this figure. The world's most successful co-financing mechanism (Gavi — the global vaccine financing body — achieves ~0.2–0.3× through a mandatory co-pay contractually enforced from recipient governments. CHAI's 2.15× from a non-binding TA program has no empirical precedent.

GiveWell's April 2025 assessment of CHAI's Technical Support Unit (TSU) grant — the only independent evaluation — found zero past examples of CHAI achieving its claimed leverage ratios, required a minimum 5.3:1 ratio to clear their bar, and flagged displacement risk.

LEN's structural advantage — only 2 clinic visits per year vs 6 for CAB-LA, vs 12+ for oral PrEP — should improve retention relative to CAB-LA's 72–75% US real-world. Post-USAID deployment landscape

Run: LEN_country_deployment_post_USAID — 3 phases, Sep 22 2026

Since 2025, PEPFAR has cut roughly 70% of HIV prevention programs across Africa [run] — the most significant structural change to LEN's rollout. Key findings:

  • ~70% of PEPFAR-funded HIV prevention programs have been discontinued across target countries. The clinic networks, data systems, and trained personnel that formed the backbone of PrEP delivery are at least partially dismantled in Nigeria, Uganda, and several other key countries.
  • Global Fund has stepped up — GF's 8th Replenishment included LEN as a priority, and the April 2026 announcement committed to country rollout. But GF funding is ~40% of PEPFAR at peak.
  • South Africa has the strongest domestic capacity and can likely sustain LEN rollout through its National Department of Health (NDoH) + Global Fund. Kenya has moderate capacity. Nigeria, Uganda, and DRC are highly PEPFAR-dependent and most at risk.
  • Gilead's voluntary licensing to 6 generic manufacturers covering 120+ LMICs ensures supply and affordable pricing regardless of PEPFAR status. The supply side is secure.
  • For CHAI's model: Pathway 2B (donor leverage) has a shrinking base to work with. CHAI's argument that the post-PEPFAR gap raises the value of optimization TA is a real efficiency argument — but it's not the same as the co-financing leverage that drives their headline CE number.
Post-USAID adjustment to CE
If PEPFAR program capacity in key countries (Nigeria, Uganda) is not replaced by GF or domestic sources, the addressable population in CHAI's model shrinks materially. A 30% reduction in reachable population × the central retention and coverage assumptions would push CE toward the $300–500/DALY range even at 0.2× leverage.

Delivery cost trajectory

Total LEN PrEP delivery cost (drug + administration + monitoring + overhead) is currently estimated at $90–160/person-year [run] in SSA depending on setting, with drug cost ($40/PY Dr Reddy's) being only ~25–45% of total.

The self-injection pathway is the highest-impact cost reduction: eliminating the clinic visit requirement for each injection would reduce delivery costs by ~30–40%. Subcutaneous self-injection of LEN is under evaluation but not yet WHO-recommended or widely available.

Break-even: At current delivery costs (~$90–125/PY), LEN PrEP CE in SSA is approximately $200–400/DALY using standard GiveWell-equivalent methodology — better than HIV treatment but not GiveWell-class. At 2030 optimistic costs ($43–62/PY with self-injection), CE could approach $80–120/DALY. The 2030 scenario requires both self-injection approval and further generic competition — plausible but not guaranteed by 2030.

CHAI's role in cost reduction: CHAI's proven market-shaping track record on drug prices (ARVs, pediatric DTG, hepatitis) is real and relevant here. The Dr Reddy's deal at $40/PY is a direct CHAI contribution. Further price reductions (toward $20/PY) and delivery simplification (self-injection protocol) are areas where CHAI TA is genuinely additive. This strengthens the market-shaping grant case, but does not support the co-financing leverage claim.

Recommendation

Fund CHAI LEN at modest scale (~$3–5M) as a market-shaping and acceleration bet, NOT as a $/DALY buy at $68/DALY.

What CHAI actually contributes:

  • The Dr Reddy's generic deal ($40/PY across 120 LMICs) — verified market-shaping outcome
  • Country-level TA for regulatory/formulary/implementation — real acceleration value
  • Post-PEPFAR optimization and gap-filling — real but declining leverage base

Gate disbursement on two specific tests:

  1. Pathway 2A test: Document at least one case of CHAI TA causing a government to allocate new domestic budget specifically for LEN (not GF/PEPFAR money routed through government systems). Target: South Africa or Botswana (both have fiscal capacity for this).
  2. Pathway 2B test: Demonstrate measurable acceleration — rollout timeline in CHAI-supported countries is at least 12 months faster than in non-CHAI-supported countries with equivalent GF allocation. Independent attribution methodology separating CHAI's contribution from other actors.

Do not credit the 2.15× multiplier in any decision calculation. Do not fund at the $68/DALY headline. The honest price is the market-shaping price.

Prior runs context (Aug 2026)
Prior deep evaluation runs in August 2026 produced a blended CE estimate of $68–157/DALY (Pathway 2 model: 2A/2B structure) vs $453–608/DALY (pure health skeptical). The September 2026 runs now specifically evaluate the leverage mechanism and find the 2.15× multiplier is unsupported. The prior runs' $453–608 range (skeptical of leverage) is closer to the evidence base. The $68–157 range requires accepting unverified leverage assumptions.
